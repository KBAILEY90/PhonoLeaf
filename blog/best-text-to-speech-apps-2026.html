What actually matters for books you already own

Your files, or their store? Some apps only read books sold through their own store. Others open the EPUB files you already have.
Natural voice, or robotic? There's a real gap between a flat system voice and a modern neural one, and it's obvious within the first sentence.
Private, or sent to a server? Cloud voices need your text to leave your device. On-device voices don't.
Flat rate, or per-book? Some pricing scales with how many books you listen to. Some doesn't.

The main categories out there

Cloud-voice reading apps like Speechify, ElevenReader, and Voice Dream Reader generate natural-sounding narration, usually on a subscription. They differ from each other in pricing and platform reach; see the individual comparison pages linked below for the specifics of each.

Free reader apps built on your phone's own voice, like @Voice Aloud Reader, cost nothing and read a wide range of file formats using whatever text-to-speech engine is already installed on your device.

Store-first reading apps like Google Play Books and Apple Books are primarily bookstores and readers, with text to speech as a secondary feature layered on top rather than the main design goal.

On-device natural-voice apps built specifically for books you already own are the newer, smaller category PhonoLeaf sits in: connect your existing library, get a natural voice generated privately on your device.
