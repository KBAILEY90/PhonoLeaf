How to listen to your Google Drive ebooks

If they're already in Drive, you're one sign-in away
Published August 19, 2026

A lot of people end up with a folder of EPUBs quietly sitting in Google Drive, and no easy way to actually listen to any of them. Here's how to close that gap.

Where those files usually come from

It's rarely one source. A Calibre export backed up to the cloud, a download from a store that doesn't lock its files, a personal digitized collection, a file a friend or family member sent along. Over a few years it adds up to a real library, just not one anything is set up to read aloud.

Connect once, not per book

  1. Sign in with Google PhonoLeaf asks for read-only access to Drive, nothing more.
  2. Choose the folder Pick the one your EPUBs live in. Every DRM-free file inside shows up with its cover art.
  3. It stays in sync Add a new book to that folder later and it appears in your library on its own, no re-import.

What stays private

PhonoLeaf's access to your Drive is read-only: it can see and download the files in the folder you choose, and nothing else. It cannot create, edit, move, or delete anything in your Drive. The natural voice that reads your books is generated on your own device, not by sending your book's text to a third-party server. There's more on that in why on-device text to speech is more private.

One folder, any source It doesn't matter whether the EPUBs came from Calibre, a bookstore export, or a decade of scattered downloads. If they're DRM-free and sitting in a Drive folder, PhonoLeaf can read them.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to move my files anywhere?

No. PhonoLeaf reads them straight from wherever they already sit in your Drive. Nothing is copied to another server.

What happens when I add a new book later?

It appears in your library on its own the next time PhonoLeaf checks the folder. No re-import step.

Can PhonoLeaf edit or delete anything in my Drive?

No. It requests read-only access, which can view and download files but cannot modify, move, or delete anything.

Does this work with subfolders?

PhonoLeaf reads the EPUBs directly inside the folder you connect. If your books are spread across several subfolders, point it at the one that holds the ones you want to listen to, or a parent folder that contains them.
