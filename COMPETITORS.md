# PhonoLeaf — Competitor analysis

Where PhonoLeaf sits, what each competitor does well and less well, and the honest
read on our strengths and gaps. Prices verified August 2026 (see `BUSINESS.md` and
`STORE_LISTINGS.md`). The key insight up front: our closest rivals are not the famous
cloud apps like Speechify, they are the on device epub readers that already let
people listen to their own books. Speechify and friends read anything but live in
the cloud; the on device readers keep it private but sound robotic. **PhonoLeaf is
the only one that is neural, private, offline, and built around your own library at
once.** That combination is the whole opportunity.

---

## 1. The three groups of competitors

- **Group A, direct: on device readers that speak your own ebooks.** @Voice Aloud
  Reader, Moon+ Reader, Legado, Voice Dream Reader, plus the free read aloud built
  into Google Play Books and Apple Books. This is the group we actually compete with.
- **Group B, broad cloud text to speech: read anything, in the cloud.** Speechify,
  NaturalReader, ElevenReader.
- **Group C, audiobook alternatives: buy the narration instead.** Audible, Libro.fm,
  Kobo and Google Play audiobooks.

## 3. Group A, the direct competitors

### @Voice Aloud Reader (the one to beat)
- **Does well:** free, mature, actively updated, reads many formats and web pages,
  works fully on device and offline, no account, huge installed base. It is the
  default answer to "how do I listen to my own ebook on Android."
- **Does less well:** it uses the phone's built in text to speech, which sounds
  robotic unless the user hunts down and installs premium system voices. The
  interface is dated and busy, it shows ads, and it is not marketed around privacy or
  a synced personal library.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** we win on voice quality (neural on device, no setup), on
  polish, and on a synced library across web and Android. They win on price (free),
  format breadth, and maturity. Our job is to make the better voice and the calmer
  experience obviously worth a few dollars.

### Moon+ Reader
- **Does well:** a powerful, beloved Android ebook reader with deep customization and
  built in text to speech, huge user base, cheap one time Pro.
- **Does less well:** text to speech is a side feature on top of the device voice,
  the app is feature heavy and complex, and it is reading first, listening second.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** we are listening first with a far better voice; they are a
  full reader that happens to talk. Different center of gravity.

### Legado (open source)
- **Does well:** free, open source, very flexible, popular for web novels, device TTS.
- **Does less well:** technical to set up, no polish, no support, niche audience.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** not a threat for mainstream users; relevant only to tinkerers.

### Voice Dream Reader
- **Does well:** the accessibility gold standard, well designed, good downloadable
  voices, imports from Drive and Dropbox, strong reputation with dyslexia and low
  vision users.
- **Does less well:** expensive at $79.99 a year, moved from a one time purchase to a
  subscription which upset long time users, historically iOS first with a weaker
  Android story, and it is DRM free epub only, the same limit we have.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** this is our most important target in the accessibility
  segment. We can be cheaper, private, truly cross platform, and on device. To win
  here we should add follow along word highlighting, which is the one accessibility
  feature they have and we do not.

### Play Books and Apple Books read aloud
- **Does well:** free, zero setup, voices are improving, already on every phone.
- **Does less well:** locked to that store's books, basic voices, not your own
  sideloaded library across ecosystems.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** they cover books bought in their store; we cover the books you
  already own from anywhere. Complementary more than competing, but they set the free
  baseline we must clearly beat.

## 4. Group B, broad cloud text to speech

### Speechify
- **Does well:** reads literally anything, including PDFs, web pages and scanned pages
  through OCR; strong premium and celebrity voices; enormous brand and marketing;
  works everywhere including a browser extension.
- **Does less well:** it is cloud based, so your text is uploaded and it needs a
  connection; it is pricey at $139 a year; it is a general tool, not built around your
  book library; and it has a reputation for aggressive upsells and billing that is
  hard to cancel.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** they are broader and louder; we are private, offline, cheaper,
  and focused on your own books. We should never try to match "reads anything," we
  should own "reads the books you own, privately."

### ElevenReader (watch this one)
- **Does well:** world class ElevenLabs voices, a free tier, reads epubs, PDFs and
  articles, is now bundling audiobooks, and is backed by a fast moving, well funded
  voice AI company.
- **Does less well:** cloud based, so the same privacy and offline gaps; it pulls you
  into the ElevenLabs ecosystem; and the free tier will tighten over time.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** the most dangerous competitor, because it is drifting toward
  our space with better voices and a free tier. Our defense is privacy, offline, your
  own library, and price. If they ever ship a strong on device or private mode, our
  edge narrows, so watch them closely.

### NaturalReader
- **Does well:** mature, OCR, many voices, cross platform, a foothold in education.
- **Does less well:** cloud based, document centric, dated experience, priced $79 to
  $159 a year.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** overlaps least with our book listening use case; more of a
  document reader.

## 5. Group C, audiobook alternatives
- **Audible, Libro.fm, store audiobooks do well:** professional human narration, which
  is still the best listening experience, and huge catalogs.
- **They do less well:** you pay for their catalog, per book or by subscription, and
  you cannot listen to the ebooks you already own. Over time it is expensive.
- **Versus PhonoLeaf:** different model. They sell you narration; we turn the books you
  already have into narration for a flat low price. We win on "your own library" and
  on cost for heavy readers, they win on narration quality for the specific titles they
  sell.

## 6. Where PhonoLeaf stands

### Real strengths
- **A position nobody else holds:** neural voice, private, offline, and built around
  your own library, all at once. The cloud apps give up privacy; the free readers give
  up voice quality. We are the only one in the middle.
- **On device neural voice** that beats the device text to speech of the free readers,
  while costing us nothing to run.
- **Zero cost to serve**, so we can price low and still profit, and offer a generous
  trial and a lifetime option.
- **Your own library, synced across web and Android**, in a calm, modern, focused app
  with habit features, versus the cluttered veteran readers.
- **Genuinely offline** now, device verified, which most neural options cannot claim.

### Honest gaps
- **Narrow input:** DRM free epubs only. Speechify reads anything and @Voice reads
  more formats plus the web. No OCR, no articles, no PDF. Book sources are Google
  Drive plus a local device folder (both shipped 2026-08-19/20, so "Drive only" is
  no longer true); Dropbox and OneDrive remain unbuilt, and each would bring its own
  OAuth app and review burden.
- **Voice is very good, not the very best:** top cloud voices from ElevenLabs and
  Speechify still sound more human than either on device model. The "better voice on
  strong devices" feature **did ship** (2026-08-08): Kokoro is offered alongside
  Piper, gated by a one time on device CPU benchmark. But the gate is set at 5.0
  GFLOPS against a Pixel 7's measured 2.47, i.e. roughly twice the reference device,
  so a large share of real phones still get Piper. Treat "Piper for most users today"
  as the working assumption until the borderline device testing in `TODO.md` gives
  the threshold a second calibration point.
- **Setup friction:** you must already own ebooks, use Google Drive, and connect a
  folder. Free readers just open a file; cloud apps paste any text.
- **A brand new brand:** no reviews, no track record, no installed base, and app store
  discovery is hard.
- **Android and web only:** no iOS yet, where Voice Dream and the cloud apps compete.
- **We are paid against strong free options,** so the neural voice, privacy and polish
  have to be obviously worth the price.

### Opportunities
- Own and repeat the one position only we hold, everywhere in our marketing.
- Take the accessibility segment from Voice Dream by being cheaper, private, and cross
  platform. Follow along highlighting **shipped 2026-08-19** and is no longer the gap
  it was here, though it covers the native neural path only, not the Web Speech
  fallback. The remaining accessibility differentiator worth building is a
  pronunciation editor, which `COMPETITOR_SWOT.md` found is the single most praised
  feature across three competitors.
- Widen input to shrink the "narrow" gap. Local file import and connect a folder
  **shipped 2026-08-19/20**; what is still open is Dropbox/OneDrive, and formats
  beyond EPUB (PDF above all).
- Lean into the French and Québec market, where there are few local competitors.
- Undercut Speechify and Voice Dream on price with a stronger privacy story.

### Threats
- The free readers and the built in platform read aloud keep improving, and if phone
  operating systems ship neural voices for free, our voice quality edge shrinks.
- ElevenReader, backed by ElevenLabs, is moving toward our space with better voices, a
  free tier, and audiobook bundling. It is the main one to watch.
- The niche is small and discovery is hard, so growth leans on SEO, communities, and
  word of mouth rather than paid reach.
- We depend on Google Drive and on passing Google's ongoing verification. Partly
  hedged since 2026-08-20: the local device folder is a second ingestion path that
  needs no Google scope at all, so a `drive.readonly` problem would no longer leave
  the app with no way to get books in. Still a real dependency, since Drive remains
  the primary path and Google sign in is required either way.

## 7. Strategic takeaways
1. **Compete on the combination, not on any single axis.** We lose a "reads anything"
   fight to Speechify and a "free" fight to @Voice. We win the "neural, private,
   offline, your own library" fight because we are the only one in it.
2. **Close the gaps that matter.** ~~Widen input (local and Dropbox) and add
   accessibility follow along highlighting.~~ **Both of those shipped in August 2026**
   (local import and connect a folder, 2026-08-19/20; follow along highlighting,
   2026-08-19). The gaps that remain, in the order the review evidence supports, are a
   **pronunciation editor**, **formats beyond EPUB** (PDF above all), and **MP3
   export**. See `COMPETITOR_SWOT.md`, which sourced all three from actual store
   reviews, and `SWOT.md`, which notes none of them is scheduled yet.
3. **Keep price low and the privacy story loud.** The zero cost to serve is our
   permission to undercut the cloud apps while out classing the free ones on voice.
   **Caveat added 2026-08-30:** `COMPETITOR_SWOT.md`'s review mining found privacy is
   why people approve but *reliability* is why they switch, so "loud privacy" is
   probably the wrong lead for the marketing copy even though it stays true. That
   reframe is tracked as its own task in `TODO.md`; flagged here so this file and that
   research do not quietly disagree.
4. **Watch ElevenReader.** If they ship private or on device, revisit our messaging and
   consider offering a top tier voice to match.
5. **Do not chase Audible.** Human narration is a different product; our pitch is the
   books you already own, for a flat low price.
