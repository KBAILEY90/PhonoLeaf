Why apps stop when the screen turns off

Most reading apps play audio as a simple sound file. When the screen locks, Android is free to suspend the app that was feeding it, and the sound stops. Getting around that needs a proper background service and a wake lock, which is more work than it sounds and is why so many apps skip it.

PhonoLeaf runs one. Playback survives the lock, the pocket and the walk, and the lock screen carries real controls rather than a dead notification.

Why apps lose your place

A position stored only in memory is gone the moment the app is killed, and a position stored per account rather than per book gets overwritten the next time you open something else.

PhonoLeaf writes your position to the device on every page turn and every time you leave the app, separately for each book. Not near where you were. Where you were.

Why books get stuck on "processing"

If an app makes the audio on a server, your book joins a queue with everyone else's. A long book means a long wait, and a busy evening means a longer one.

PhonoLeaf makes the voice on your phone, one sentence ahead of the one you are hearing. There is no queue to join, so there is nothing to wait for.

Why the voice gets worse on a weak connection

Cloud voices degrade deliberately. When bandwidth drops, the app falls back to a lower-quality voice so that something keeps playing. It is a sensible engineering decision that sounds terrible.

A voice generated on the device has nothing to degrade to. It is the same voice on five bars, one bar, and none.

Questions people actually ask

Why does my audiobook app stop when the screen turns off?

Usually because it plays audio without a proper background service, so the system suspends it when the screen locks. PhonoLeaf runs a real foreground service with a wake lock, so playback continues.

Why do reading apps lose my place in a book?

Because the position is held in memory, or stored in one place shared across books. PhonoLeaf saves it to your device on every page turn, separately for each book, down to the paragraph.

Does PhonoLeaf work without an internet connection?

Yes. Save a book for offline listening and it keeps reading with no connection at all, including generating new audio as it goes.

Why does my ebook get stuck on "processing"?

Because the audio is being generated on a server and your book is in a queue. PhonoLeaf generates the voice on your phone as it reads, so there is no queue.

Does the voice quality drop on a weak connection?

No. The voice is generated on your device, so there is no streaming quality to fall back from.
